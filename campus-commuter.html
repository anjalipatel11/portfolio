01

Project Overview

As a part of our INST362 course (User Centered Design), we were tasked with addressing an existing problem college students face and finding an improved user-centered solution.

We chose to address the issue of campus transportation — inconsistencies with the existing Transit app that UMD uses. Students, specifically commuters, struggle with transportation information, parking availability, and campus congestion.

Campus Commute explores how user-centered design could improve the daily commuting experience for UMD students. Through interviews, qualitative analysis, collaborative ideation, and iterative wireframing, our team identified key pain points and developed concepts that prioritized convenience, clarity, and accessibility.

02

Problem & Solution

Problem

The existing UMD Transit app only allows users to track the buses and their routes. This only serves those who use the bus, but what about commuters who drive to campus, students who walk to class, or ride their E-scooters across campus? Wouldn't a more encompassing Transit app be beneficial to all students on campus?

Solution

An app that includes bus routes and schedules, real-time parking availability, and alerts about on campus events, road closures, and construction that may cause traffic patterns to change.

Change the way UMD approaches transportation services to make a more efficient and informative experience for all.

Design Challenge

We began with the design challenge by addressing the initial problem, needs of the clients, and possible systems we'd need to work with.

We organized what we believe, who for, what we hope to achieve, and how we'll know if we're right.

Research Planning

We planned our research approach around the transportation experiences of UMD students. This helped us determine what information we needed to gather before beginning the design process.

10 + 10 Design Thinking

We used 10 + 10 Design Thinking for visual brainstorming to generate a wide range of ideas before narrowing them down.

We did this twice to determine the most important and specific ideas we wanted to address.

Affinity Diagram

We then organized our brainstorming into meaningful categories:

  • Transportation Forms
  • Issues
  • Reasoning

We also considered the overall goal and how that would influence our app design.

Qualitative Data Analysis

Using our survey and interview responses, we organized the user's concerns using Open and Axial Coding. We uncovered several recurring concerns and frustrations students experience with transportation on campus.

01

Open Coding

With Open Coding, we tagged individual user responses, which led us to identify five key categories: driving/commuting concerns, biking/scootering concerns, pedestrian issues, and bus-related problems.

These open codes allowed us to recognize not only the logistical issues but also the emotional and experiential dimensions of campus travel — ranging from fear and confusion to inefficiencies and delays.

02

Axial Coding

The Axial Coding phase helped us refine and prioritize these themes. By organizing concerns along a spectrum from least to most critical, we found that driving/commuting concerns ranked highest in urgency, followed closely by biking/scootering issues and pedestrian safety.

Understanding the Users

We began with talking to UMD students to better understand our users and their needs. We conducted surveys and interviews to propose our idea and gain feedback from a range of students — commuters, off-campus/on-campus students, and students who walk, scooter, or use the bus.

We used their feedback on the existing Transit app to better gauge our approach to a solution.

06

Reflection

This project taught me the importance of letting research guide design decisions rather than making assumptions about user needs. Working through each stage of the UX process from interviews and affinity mapping, to ideation and wireframing, showed me how meaningful results can come through structured analysis. It strengthened my passion for designing with users and their needs, and not just for them.

Next Steps

The next phase of this project would be to transform the low-fidelity wireframes into a polished, interactive prototype that aligns with the current aesthetics of the existing Transit app while incorporating the proposed features.

This would be followed by usability testing with student commuters to validate design decisions, identify pain points, and refine the experience through iterative improvements.
